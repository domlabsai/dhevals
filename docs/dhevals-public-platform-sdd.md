# DHEvals Public Platform — Software Design Document

**Status:** Design baseline for implementation
**Version:** 0.1
**Audience:** Product, design, frontend, data, and evaluation engineering
**Primary language:** English for the public product; benchmark content may be
  Portuguese (Brazil) when the suite is pt-BR.
**Product surface:** Public web platform and shareable benchmark reports

## 1. Executive summary

DHEvals Public Platform is the public-facing layer of DHEvals: a fast,
evidence-first destination where people can discover model results, inspect the
benchmark behind a score, compare models head to head, and share a trustworthy
result on X or LinkedIn.

The product should feel more like an independent research publication and a
high-signal data instrument than a generic SaaS dashboard. The visual language
inherits the existing DHEvals “midnight lab console”: near-black surfaces,
acid-lime evidence states, restrained cobalt comparison states, hairline data
separators, and dense but calm typography. The public experience adds an
editorial layer inspired by modern model leaderboards: a discovery homepage,
shareable filtered tables, model profiles, benchmark pages, comparison views,
methodology, and report permalinks.

The first release is read-only. It consumes verified, publication-safe artifacts
generated by the existing DHEvals runner and does not expose credentials,
private prompts, reviewer identity, or unpromoted runs.

## 2. Product definition

### 2.1 Positioning

> DHEvals shows how AI models behave when the work is real, multi-step, and
> worth checking.

### 2.2 Primary jobs to be done

1. **Discover:** Find a useful answer to “which model performs well for this
   workload?” without reading a research paper first.
2. **Verify:** Follow a score to its suite, task coverage, evidence status,
   scoring method, and run artifact.
3. **Compare:** See where two models differ in quality, reliability, cost,
   latency, context, and coverage.
4. **Share:** Send a URL or a social card that preserves the exact filter,
   comparison, result, and date being discussed.
5. **Learn:** Understand the benchmark design and limitations before drawing a
   conclusion.
6. **Publish:** Promote a verified run without mutating the public baseline by
   accident.

### 2.3 Success criteria

- A first-time visitor can understand what DHEvals measures in under 20
  seconds.
- A visitor can reach a model result from the homepage in two interactions.
- Every public score has a visible evidence state and a path to methodology.
- A comparison URL is deterministic, canonical, and shareable.
- The same page remains legible in a 16:9 screen recording and on a mobile
  device.
- Public pages contain no secrets, private prompts, unreviewed personal data,
  or unverified scores.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### 3.1 Goals for v1

- Public leaderboard with quality, evidence, and operational metrics.
- Search and filtering by model, provider, benchmark, category, license,
  evidence state, and release status.
- Model profile pages with score history and workload breakdown.
- Two-model comparison with absolute scores and deltas.
- Benchmark and methodology pages that explain the evaluation contract.
- Run/report pages with task-level evidence and verification metadata.
- Share cards and metadata for X, LinkedIn, Slack, and generic Open Graph.
- Static-first delivery from checked-in or generated public artifacts.
- Keyboard-accessible, high-contrast, reduced-motion experience.

### 3.2 Explicit non-goals for v1

- User accounts, comments, voting, or community-submitted scores.
- Live model inference from the browser.
- Exposing provider API keys or private evaluation prompts.
- A generic chat playground.
- A proprietary composite score that hides raw dimensions.
- Automatic promotion of every completed run to the public leaderboard.
- A full CMS; release metadata remains versioned and reviewable in the repo.

## 4. Users and audience

### 4.1 Heavy user / builder

Needs to choose a model for research, coding, planning, analysis, or agentic
work. Wants task-level detail, not a single marketing number.

### 4.2 Researcher / evaluator

Needs to inspect suite versions, rubric dimensions, provenance, coverage, and
limitations. Values reproducibility and explicit uncertainty.

### 4.3 Engineering or procurement decision-maker

Needs a quick comparison of quality, price, latency, context, and availability,
then a link to the evidence for internal discussion.

### 4.4 Social audience

Arrives from X, LinkedIn, or a video. Needs a strong headline, one clear
result, a visible “how this was measured” path, and a readable mobile layout.

### 4.5 Internal publisher

Runs the evaluator, reviews verification and calibration gates, and promotes a
release. This role is an operational workflow, not a public account type in v1.

## 5. Product principles

1. **Evidence is the interface.** Scores, coverage, freshness, and verification
   are always close to the claim.
2. **Separate quality from confidence.** A high score with thin evidence must
   not look identical to a high score with broad evidence.
3. **Raw facts before composite narratives.** Keep task results, dimensions,
   units, and denominators inspectable.
4. **Shareable by default.** Filters, comparisons, and report views must have
   stable URLs and intentional social previews.
5. **Density with hierarchy.** Show more useful information without turning
   every section into a grid of identical cards.
6. **Quiet confidence.** Use motion, color, and typography to guide attention;
   never use spectacle to hide uncertainty.
7. **Archive before promotion.** A run can be real and still remain archive-only
   until the publication contract is satisfied.
8. **The video frame is a first-class viewport.** Important content must read at
   16:9 capture size without requiring tiny browser chrome or a zoom.

## 6. Information architecture

### 6.1 Global navigation

Desktop navigation is a compact top bar with the DHEvals lockup on the left and
the following primary destinations:

- **Leaderboard** — ranked models and filters.
- **Compare** — model-to-model analysis.
- **Benchmarks** — suites, tasks, categories, and dimensions.
- **Reports** — published runs and result stories.
- **Methodology** — scoring, calibration, evidence, and limitations.

Secondary actions: search, theme (dark-first with an optional light mode), data
download, and an “About DHEvals” link in the footer. On mobile, the top bar
becomes a compact header with a bottom or sheet navigation.

### 6.2 Route map

| Route | Purpose | Indexable | Shareable |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `/` | Editorial overview and current signal | Yes | Yes |
| `/leaderboard` | Ranked model table | Yes | Yes |
| `/leaderboard?category=coding&status=supported` | Filtered ranking | Yes, canonicalized | Yes |
| `/models/:modelSlug` | Model profile and history | Yes | Yes |
| `/compare/:modelA-vs-:modelB` | Head-to-head comparison | Yes | Yes |
| `/benchmarks` | Suite and category index | Yes | Yes |
| `/benchmarks/:suiteSlug` | Suite detail and methodology | Yes | Yes |
| `/reports` | Published report index | Yes | Yes |
| `/reports/:runId` | Verified run and task evidence | Yes | Yes |
| `/methodology` | Trust, score, and calibration explanation | Yes | Yes |
| `/about` | Project, contact, and contribution context | Yes | Yes |
| `/data` | Downloadable JSON/CSV index | Yes | Yes |

### 6.3 URL state contract

Filter state must be encoded in query parameters, sorted deterministically, and
preserved when a user copies the URL. Use short, readable keys:

```text
/leaderboard?category=research,coding&provider=openai&status=supported
/leaderboard?sort=quality_desc&suite=v0.2&license=open
```

Unknown parameters are ignored rather than causing a broken page. A canonical
URL omits default values and uses stable model and suite slugs.

## 7. Page specifications

### 7.1 Homepage `/`

The homepage is an editorial entry point, not a dashboard dump.

**Above the fold**

- DHEvals lockup and one-line promise.
- “Latest verified signal” block with date, suite, model, score, and evidence
  state.
- Primary CTA: **Explore leaderboard**.
- Secondary CTA: **See methodology**.
- A small live/recent indicator that never implies live inference if the data is
  static.

**Below the fold**

- Three decision cards: best overall verified, strongest for a workload, and
  best value when data is available. Every card links to evidence.
- Category strip or compact heatmap for research, coding, reasoning, analysis,
  writing, and tool use.
- “How DHEvals works” four-step sequence: choose suite → run model → verify
  evidence → publish result.
- Recent reports with timestamps and suite version.
- Methodology teaser and limitations.
- Social-ready footer with GitHub, X, LinkedIn, and data links.

### 7.2 Leaderboard `/leaderboard`

The leaderboard is the primary utility surface.

**Table columns**

1. Rank (only when a valid ranking scope exists).
2. Model and provider.
3. Evidence state (`Supported`, `Estimated`, `Pending`, `Locked`).
4. Overall quality score (0–100) with a visible scale.
5. Workload/category score selected by the current view.
6. Cost or price unit when available, with exact units.
7. Latency or throughput when measured.
8. Context window when known.
9. Last verified date.

The table must support search, provider, category, license, evidence, suite,
date, and sort filters. Mobile uses a compact model row with a details drawer;
it must not simply shrink nine desktop columns into unreadable text.

### 7.3 Model profile `/models/:modelSlug`

- Model name, provider, release/status, license, and source links.
- Score hero with quality and evidence confidence as separate values.
- Workload radar or horizontal bars, with a table alternative for accessibility.
- Cost, latency, throughput, context, and availability cards only when measured.
- Run history with suite version and verification date.
- Strengths and limitations generated from explicit data, never invented by the
  UI.
- Primary actions: **Compare**, **View latest run**, **Download data**.

### 7.4 Comparison `/compare/:modelA-vs-:modelB`

- Clear “A vs B” title and model identity blocks.
- Decision summary: where A leads, where B leads, and where data is tied or
  missing.
- Absolute scores and deltas for quality, coverage, latency, cost, and each
  workload category.
- Task heatmap with a legend and accessible table view.
- Evidence coverage and suite version shown before the conclusion.
- Copy/share action that produces an exact comparison URL and social card.

The page must never present a winner when the compared suite, sample, or
evidence state is not comparable.

### 7.5 Benchmarks `/benchmarks` and `/benchmarks/:suiteSlug`

- Index of suite versions with status, task count, dimensions, language,
  last-reviewed date, and hash.
- Detail page with benchmark purpose, task categories, scoring dimensions,
  deterministic checks, human calibration state, limitations, and example
  task shapes.
- A visible distinction between fixture, calibrated, verified, and promoted
  data.
- Link to the exact manifest and downloadable machine-readable data.

### 7.6 Reports `/reports` and `/reports/:runId`

The report page is the long-form evidence surface used after a social click.

- Run header: model, provider, suite, version, date, status, and verification.
- Score overview with coverage and explicit “archive-only” or “public” state.
- Task list with score, status, latency, tokens, and failure reason.
- Inspector or expandable details for prompt context, response evidence,
  checks, and sources, subject to publication policy.
- Export links for JSON, CSV, HTML, and YouTube summary.
- A provenance panel with manifest hash, runner version, and artifact links.

### 7.7 Methodology `/methodology`

Explain the trust model in plain language and then provide technical depth:

- What is measured and what is not.
- Deterministic score calculation.
- Coverage, evidence state, and calibration gate.
- Why quality and cost are separate.
- How failures, timeouts, and missing data are represented.
- How to reproduce a run locally.
- Known limitations and a changelog of methodology versions.

## 8. Core data contract

The public UI consumes a normalized read model. Source run artifacts remain
immutable; a build step creates the public projection.

### 8.1 Model

```json
{
  "id": "provider/model-slug",
  "name": "Model name",
  "provider": "Provider",
  "status": "current",
  "license": "open|closed|unknown",
  "capabilities": ["coding", "research"],
  "quality_score": 82.4,
  "evidence_status": "supported",
  "evidence_coverage": 0.86,
  "last_verified_at": "2026-08-03T12:00:00Z",
  "metrics": {
    "input_cost_per_1k": null,
    "output_cost_per_1k": null,
    "latency_ms": null,
    "tokens_per_second": null,
    "context_tokens": 1048576
  },
  "sources": []
}
```

### 8.2 Suite

```json
{
  "id": "heavy-user-ptbr",
  "version": "v0.2",
  "slug": "heavy-user-ptbr-v0-2",
  "title": "Heavy-user tasks — Brazilian Portuguese",
  "language": "pt-BR",
  "task_count": 10,
  "dimension_count": 14,
  "status": "calibration_pending",
  "manifest_hash": "sha256:...",
  "last_reviewed_at": null
}
```

### 8.3 Public run

```json
{
  "id": "run-2026-08-03-model-v02",
  "model_id": "provider/model-slug",
  "suite_id": "heavy-user-ptbr@v0.2",
  "run_status": "verified|archive_only|promoted|invalid",
  "quality_score": 66.67,
  "coverage": 1.0,
  "verified": true,
  "started_at": "2026-08-03T12:00:00Z",
  "completed_at": "2026-08-03T12:12:00Z",
  "artifacts": {
    "json": "/reports/runs/run-2026-08-03-model-v02.json",
    "report": "/reports/runs/run-2026-08-03-model-v02.html",
    "csv": "/reports/runs/run-2026-08-03-model-v02.csv"
  }
}
```

### 8.4 Evidence states

| State | Meaning | Ranking behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `supported` | Enough direct, diverse, verified evidence | Eligible |
| `estimated` | Visible but evidence is incomplete or indirect | Visible with uncertainty |
| `pending` | Run or calibration is not complete | Not ranked |
| `locked` | Artifact exists but promotion gate is closed | Not ranked |
| `invalid` | Verification failed or required evidence is missing | Hidden from public ranking |

## 9. Scoring and trust semantics

- Quality is represented on a 0–100 scale and preserves the underlying
  deterministic score.
- Coverage is a separate 0–1 value and must be shown when a score is partial.
- `not_evaluated`, `pending`, and `locked` are states, not zeros.
- Cost, latency, throughput, and context are operational metrics, never hidden
  quality weights.
- Composite rankings disclose the scope, category weights, suite version, and
  evidence threshold.
- A tie or incomparable result must be rendered as such; the UI may not infer a
  winner from missing data.

## 10. System architecture

### 10.1 V1 static-first architecture

```text
Model CLI / HTTP endpoint
          │
          ▼
   DHEvals runner ──► immutable run artifact
          │
          ▼
 verify + audit + calibration + release gate
          │
          ▼
 public projection builder (JSON/CSV/HTML)
          │
          ▼
 React/Vite public platform on CDN
```

The browser never calls a model endpoint. Public pages read a versioned static
projection or a read-only API generated from that projection.

### 10.2 Future data service

A future API may expose the same read model from Postgres/Supabase or another
provider-neutral store. The frontend must depend on the public schema, not on a
vendor SDK, so the static and hosted modes remain interchangeable.

### 10.3 Recommended frontend boundaries

- `app-shell`: navigation, theme, global search, footer.
- `public-data`: schema validation, fetch/cache, URL state.
- `leaderboard`: table, filters, sorting, row actions.
- `model-profile`: score hero, metrics, category breakdown, history.
- `comparison`: delta calculations, heatmap, evidence guardrails.
- `benchmark-catalog`: suite index and detail.
- `report-viewer`: run header, task table, inspector, exports.
- `methodology`: content sections and interactive score explainer.
- `share-cards`: deterministic social card data and previews.

## 11. API and static data contract

V1 may serve static JSON files, but the following resource shapes are normative:

| Resource | Read endpoint | Cache policy |
|---|---|---|
| Overview | `/api/public/overview` | 5 minutes |
| Leaderboard | `/api/public/leaderboard` | 5 minutes |
| Model | `/api/public/models/:id` | 1 hour |
| Comparison | `/api/public/compare?a=&b=&suite=` | 5 minutes |
| Suites | `/api/public/suites` | 1 hour |
| Runs | `/api/public/runs/:id` | Immutable |
| Reports | `/api/public/reports` | 5 minutes |
| Methodology | `/api/public/methodology` | 1 day |
| Health | `/api/health` | No cache |

All responses include `schema_version`, `generated_at`, and `source_revision`.
Errors use a stable shape:

```json
{
  "error": {
    "code": "PUBLIC_DATA_UNAVAILABLE",
    "message": "The requested public artifact is not available.",
    "request_id": "req_..."
  }
}
```

## 12. Search, filters, and shareability

- Search must match model name, provider, suite, category, and report title.
- Search results include type, evidence state, and last verified date.
- Filters are URL-backed and preserve ordering when copied.
- Every model, suite, report, and comparison has a canonical URL.
- Share actions expose `Copy link`, `Copy X text`, and `Download card`.
- Social text must state the scope and date, for example: “Model A scored
  74.2/100 on Heavy-user pt-BR v0.2 — 10 tasks, verified Aug 3, 2026.”
- Never generate “best model” copy when the current scope is filtered or the
  evidence gate is not eligible.

## 13. SEO and social distribution

Every indexable page needs:

- title under 60 characters where possible;
- description under 160 characters;
- canonical URL;
- Open Graph image at 1200×630;
- `twitter:card=summary_large_image`;
- JSON-LD for `Dataset`, `SoftwareApplication`, `Article`, or `BreadcrumbList`
  where appropriate;
- `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, and stable `lastmod` values;
- a visible “last verified” date and source link.

Social cards must contain the DHEvals mark, one primary claim, the suite/model
scope, the evidence state, and the verification date. They must not contain
private prompts or unverified ranking language.

## 14. Performance and reliability requirements

- LCP ≤ 2.5 seconds on a warm CDN and ≤ 3.5 seconds on a mid-tier mobile
  connection for the homepage.
- First interaction ready ≤ 3 seconds on the leaderboard route.
- Static artifact pages work when the API is unavailable.
- Table filters respond in under 100 ms after data is loaded.
- No chart animation blocks reading or interaction.
- Public JSON and report artifacts use immutable hashes where possible.
- Build fails when a public artifact fails schema validation.
- Build fails when a promoted run is not verified.

## 15. Accessibility requirements

- WCAG 2.2 AA target.
- All controls are keyboard reachable and have visible focus.
- Score, status, and evidence never rely on color alone.
- Tables have semantic headers, captions, and a mobile alternative.
- Charts expose an adjacent data table or textual summary.
- Drawers and dialogs trap focus and return focus to their trigger.
- Text meets contrast requirements on the darkest and raised surfaces.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes reveal, pulse, and parallax behavior.
- Portuguese benchmark content uses correct locale formatting for dates and
  decimals; the interface remains understandable in English.

## 16. Security and privacy

- No model/API credential enters the browser bundle or public projection.
- Private prompts, raw secrets, internal file paths, and reviewer identity are
  excluded by a publication sanitizer.
- Public artifacts are validated against an allow-list of fields.
- Run IDs and source revisions are safe to expose; environment variables are
  not.
- Download endpoints use immutable public artifacts and do not execute user
  input.
- If analytics are added, use privacy-preserving aggregate events and provide
  an opt-out; never record prompt or response contents.

## 17. Observability and content operations

Track only operational, non-content signals:

- page view and route;
- filter/comparison usage;
- report download and share-card generation;
- frontend error code and release revision;
- build/promotion status.

Each public build records `source_revision`, `generated_at`, and the artifact
manifest. The publisher should review a release checklist before deployment:

1. suite hash and model identity are present;
2. verification is valid;
3. calibration/release gate status is shown correctly;
4. no private fields survive sanitization;
5. homepage, leaderboard, comparison, report, and methodology pages render;
6. social cards contain the intended title, score, scope, and date.

## 18. Testing strategy

### Unit and contract tests

- Validate public schemas and evidence-state transitions.
- Test score, delta, coverage, and missing-data rendering.
- Test URL query parsing and canonicalization.
- Test social metadata generation and deterministic card inputs.

### Integration tests

- Load a fixture projection and render every route.
- Verify a promoted run appears in the correct leaderboard scope.
- Verify an archive-only or invalid run cannot appear as ranked.
- Verify downloads point to the expected immutable artifacts.

### Browser E2E

- Homepage → leaderboard → model profile.
- Leaderboard filter → copy URL → reload preserves state.
- Model A/B comparison on desktop and mobile.
- Report inspector, evidence, source list, and export links.
- Keyboard navigation and reduced-motion mode.
- Social preview route with deterministic metadata.

### Visual regression

Capture at 1440×900, 1280×720, 1024×768, 390×844, and 1200×630 social-card
size. Review dark contrast, table density, score hierarchy, logo legibility,
and long model names.

## 19. Release plan

### Phase 1 — public shell

Landing page, navigation, brand assets, leaderboard fixture, responsive layout,
SEO defaults, and share-card template.

### Phase 2 — evidence surfaces

Model profiles, benchmark catalog, report detail, methodology, download index,
and verification states.

### Phase 3 — comparison and publishing

Head-to-head comparison, URL-backed filters, social copy generation, build
promotion checks, and public run history.

### Phase 4 — live data service

Read-only API, cached queries, release webhooks, privacy-preserving analytics,
and optional provider-backed ingestion.

## 20. Acceptance criteria

The v1 public platform is accepted when:

- all routes in the route map have a designed loading, empty, error, and ready
  state;
- the homepage communicates the product and links to a verified result;
- the leaderboard can filter and sort without losing URL state;
- model profiles and comparisons show quality and evidence separately;
- report pages expose task-level evidence and verification metadata;
- static artifacts render without a model endpoint or browser secret;
- social cards are legible at 1200×630 and contain no private data;
- keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, and reduced-motion checks pass;
- the release build blocks invalid or unverified public promotion;
- a complete 16:9 capture can be recorded without horizontal overflow or tiny
  unreadable body text.

## 21. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Composite score is mistaken for universal intelligence | Show scope, categories, coverage, and limitations next to every score |
| Fresh model data becomes stale | Show last verified date and evidence state; keep update history |
| Social sharing strips context | Deterministic OG cards include model, suite, score, date, and evidence |
| Dense table overwhelms mobile users | Use a focused row and details drawer with an accessible table fallback |
| Run artifact leaks private data | Allow-list projection plus publication sanitizer and CI test |
| Visual polish drifts from the console | Treat the design system and logo assets as versioned source of truth |
| Live API outage breaks the public site | Static-first artifacts, CDN cache, and explicit stale-data state |

## 22. Reference patterns

The information architecture intentionally studies two public leaderboard
patterns:

- [BenchLM.ai](https://benchlm.ai/) — model discovery, evidence labels,
  methodology, freshness, shareable filters, and decision-ready views.
- [LLM Stats](https://llm-stats.com/) — leaderboard navigation, comparisons,
  category paths, model profiles, and operational columns for speed, price, and
  context.

These are interaction references, not assets or copy to be reproduced. DHEvals
must retain its own evidence contract, logo, palette, and editorial voice.
